# carmen-esp32

A standalone ESP32-S3 version of the Carmen game (`../carmen`, github.com/jonorl/carmen), played on a 320x240 ILI9341 touch screen with a piezo. A separate project: never change the game's repository from here. Files taken from the game are copies, listed with the commit they came from under "Copied from Carmen" in `README.md`; update that list when copying more.

Hardware, wiring and commands are in `README.md`. `docs/wiring.svg`, the wiring diagram, is drawn by `tools/wiring.py` from the same pins as the README's table: change both together.

## Files

- `src/lib.rs`: the library the programs share; `src/main.rs` and `src/bin/*` use it as `carmen_esp32::...`.
- `src/main.rs`: the game: sets up the hardware, loads the data, and feeds each touch (press, then release once the panel reads nothing `RELEASED` times in a row) to `App`, redrawing after each.
- `src/app.rs`: the game's screens as one state machine (`App`, `Panel`): headquarters with the on-screen keyboard (`KEYS`), the briefing, the case (notes, flights, places, crime computer, where a tap steps a field to its next value), the ending and the next case. `press` highlights, `release` acts, `draw` paints the whole screen from the state. The desktop's own lines over its animations (the henchman, the warnings) are said as text here.
- `src/ui.rs`: layout A (`CLOCK`, `PICTURE`, `TEXT`, `BUTTONS`), the EGA colours, frames, the button icons (Carmen's, narrowed to half its buttons' width and drawn light on dark), and `Pager`, which pages wrapped `Row`s through the text panel.
- `src/scenes.rs`: the animated moments in the text panel (`Stage`): walking to a witness, henchmen, warnings, a flight, the crime computer, the chase, the walk to jail, the jail. Each has its length, its sound cues and its drawing, timed as Carmen's `screens/panel.rs`. `App` plays them as `Panel::Scene` before showing what happened; a tap skips a stage. `main.rs` ticks them about 30 times a second and sends only what changed (`Redraw`: the text panel, the picture frame or both, through `Display::blit_part`).
- `src/art/sprites.rs`: the original's sprites (`assets/sprites`, turned into raw RGBA by `build.rs`), animated as Carmen's `art/sprites.rs` does but at one screen pixel per original pixel (the desktop doubles them).
- The picture frame (`App::draw_picture`) follows the panel: the three places stacked (`draw_places`, each picture's drawn rows centred in a third of the frame, since three full 68-row pictures don't fit; tappable like the rows), the departure map (`draw_map`, tappable cities; during the flight the plane crosses it), the crime computer over its printer (`Panel::Crime` and the `Compute` stage, whose lights flash), the dossier photo (a drawn portrait if there's none), and at the end of a won case the printer with the promotion. Otherwise the postcard. A witness talking (`App::witness`, set by an interview, not at the hideout) stands in the text panel's bottom left corner at half size (33x48, each pixel a 2x2 block's mean) with their job beside them (`draw_witness`), and the story pages above them (`story_area`), as the original showed them; the owner didn't want them over the postcard.
- Headquarters (`App::at_hq`: signing in, checking, and the briefing before the first city) shows `App::hq_printer` in the picture frame, as the desktop's: the title and welcome, the name echoed as it's typed, "identified", then from `new_case` the rank and the case (and "welcome back" for the next case). The briefing itself stays in the text panel, where it's readable.
- The clock box shows `App::shown_t`, which `tick_clock` moves an hour at a time towards `game.t`, `HOUR_SECONDS` each, half that asleep or in `App::flown` (the last flight's hours), with `sound::TICK` for each except during a scene (the piezo plays one sound at a time, and a tick would cut its tune). It's red when the deadline is 12 hours or less away. `Redraw` is a set of parts (panel, picture, clock), so `main.rs` sends only those.
- `src/art/places.rs`, `src/art/people.rs`: copied from Carmen (see the README): `places::picture` and `people::witness`, `people::suspect_portrait` paint one 104x68 picture or 66x96 portrait on request.
- `src/art/map.rs`: Carmen's `MapView` into a `Canvas`. `Land::load` reads `$OUT_DIR/land.bin`, which `build.rs` packs from `assets/ne_110m_land.geojson` (parsing the GeoJSON on the device would make thousands of small allocations from internal RAM). The app keeps the land and the map for the current city once made.
- `src/art/computer.rs`: Carmen's crime computer redrawn 84 px tall for the top of the picture frame; returns its screen for the caller's text.
- `src/art/printer.rs`: after Carmen's printer: `print` queues text wrapped to the paper (`text_width`), `update` prints 8 whole lines a second (the owner wanted a quick line-by-line effect, not letters), `draw` fills a rectangle with paper and the printer's case. Text in Pixelify 11 (VT323 only comes at 25, too big). A COMPUTE's verdict prints there (`Scene::print`) instead of in the panel, and the panel goes back to the computer. `sound::PRINT` sounds for each frame a line comes out on.
- `src/art/postcard.rs`: the landmarks are drawn at fixed positions for a 274x282 frame, so `fitted` paints at that size and shrinks to the picture frame (averaging), repeating the sky's top row above. `windowed` (crisp, cut) is only for comparing in `bin/postcards`.
- `src/device/sound.rs`: the piezo on GPIO 4 through LEDC, from a thread fed by a channel; a new sound cuts off the last. Notes are folded by octaves into 500 Hz to 5 kHz (the timer can't make much under 300 Hz at 8 bits). Effects (key, tick, footsteps, clank) are written there; the tunes are `tunes.rs`, generated by `tools/transcribe.py` (don't edit by hand). On/off is kept in flash (`sound`), but nothing in the game switches it yet.
- `src/device/progress.rs`: arrests per name in flash (`progress`), names matched as the game's shared crate does (trimmed, lower case, at most `MAX_NAME`), and kept on the bureau's server as Carmen's `device/progress.rs` does: a look-up at sign-in takes the higher tally and always sends it back (that files a new detective), each arrest sends its tally, and the server never lowers one. `poll` turns answers into `News` for `App::poll`; signing in waits for the look-up at most `CHECK_SECONDS` (`Panel::Checking`), the roster at most `ROSTER_SECONDS` before showing this device's records.
- `src/device/net.rs`: WiFi and HTTPS on their own thread, as the pollution firmware connects (WPA2, no modem sleep, 5 attempts, rejoin before each request; ESP-IDF's certificate bundle). Requests are queued and answered by number, so the game never waits. Settings from `cfg.toml` (gitignored) through `toml-cfg`: `wifi_ssid`, `wifi_psk`, `api_url` (empty: no server).
- Roster and files: `Panel::Roster` (from the button at the bottom of headquarters' picture frame, or the right half of the crime computer's last row; a tap at its last page goes back) and `Panel::Dossier` (the left half; the photo at double size in the picture frame via `sprites::photo`, a tap goes through the file and on to the next suspect). Photos are `assets/suspects/*.png`, turned into RGBA by `build.rs` and matched by `photo_name` as Carmen does.
- `src/display.rs`: the screen through the `mipidsi` crate, landscape (`Rotation::Deg90`), RGB (BGR swapped red and blue), 40 MHz, no DMA. `blit` and `fill` take `[u8; 3]` colours.
- `src/touch.rs`: the XPT2046 on the same SPI bus (own chip select, 2 MHz). `Calibration::MEASURED` maps raw readings to screen pixels.
- `src/art/`, `src/rules/`, `src/lang.rs`, `countries.json`, `suspects.json`, `assets/fonts/`: copied from the game (see the README for what was changed in the copies).
- `build.rs`: links ESP-IDF, and turns `assets/fonts` into bitmaps (`$OUT_DIR/fonts.rs`). Pixelify Sans and VT323 are pixel fonts drawn on grids that don't start at zero (Pixelify: 91-unit cells from x 60, y -12; VT323: 40-unit cells from 0, measured with fontTools), so each glyph is rasterised large and every grid cell lit if covered enough, giving the font's own pixels. Pixelify's acute and grave accents are drawn in half-cell steps and are kept by a lower threshold for ink away from the letter (`MARK_LIT`). Change the sizes there (`PIXEL_FONTS`'s `sizes` and `smooth`).
- `src/text.rs`: `Font::new(Family, size)` (body text: `PixelifySmooth` 13; labels and headings: `Pixelify` 11 or 22, its own pixels; printer: `Vt323` 25) measures, wraps and draws onto a `Canvas`; Noto Sans stands in for ₹.
- `src/device/storage.rs`: the game's `storage::read` and `write`, kept in NVS flash; `storage::start` must run first.
- `src/bin/`: checks, `pins` (wiring), `screen` (mipidsi's test image), `touch` (calibration and drawing), `case` (the rules playing a case, printed to the serial monitor), `text` (the fonts), `layout` (the two layouts that were chosen between: A won), `postcards` (the postcards fitted and windowed), `sound` (a scale, then every effect and tune).

## Learnt on the hardware

- No soldering: any part suggested for this project must connect without it (breadboard pins already fitted, screw terminals, USB or barrel plugs). Check how a part connects before recommending it.

- A hand-written ILI9341 driver drew corrupted pictures on this module (orientation and colour settings ignored) where `mipidsi` drew it right on the same wiring. Draw through `mipidsi`; the cause in the hand-written one was never found.
- DMA on the SPI bus is untested since then: turn it on alone and check the pictures.
- T_IRQ goes low only now and then on presses, so touches are detected by pressure (`MIN_PRESSURE`), not by T_IRQ.
- The touch panel is turned 90 degrees to the picture: raw X runs down the screen and raw Y across it (`swap: true`).
- Taps need debouncing: one press read as two taps until a release had to last 0.3 s.
- The board's 5V pin is input only: USB power doesn't come out of it (that needs the IN-OUT pad bridged, which is soldering). So the screen can't take 5V from the board; one external 5V supply feeds both the board's 5V pin and the screen's VCC (jumper J1 open), with all grounds joined. The LED pin on 3V3 is already full brightness. The panel looks washed out off-axis.
- The MB102 breadboard supply can't run this from 12V: its linear 5V regulator overheats within a minute (about 7 V x 0.4 A) and the board crashes. Use a 5V supply directly, or a switching (buck) converter.

- `tools/emulator/`: records `docs/demo.gif` on the computer. It compiles the game's own modules by `#[path]` (`app`, `art`, `rules`, `scenes`, `text`, `ui`, `lang`, and `device::progress` and `tunes`) with stand-ins for the rest (`src/device/mod.rs` there: storage in memory, no network, the effects' notes cut out of `sound.rs` by its `build.rs`), and builds the fonts, sprites and coastlines with the device's `build.rs` (`assets()`, run from the repository's root). It has its own toolchain (stable) and target (the computer), since the repository's are the ESP32's. The script plays seed `SEED`'s case as Jon, a Rookie, knowing where to go from its own copy of the game sent the same moves; tap positions copy `app.rs`'s layout by hand, so update them if that changes. Rerun it after visible changes.

## Commands

- `cargo run --release`: build, flash and monitor the game.
- `cargo run --release --bin pins|screen|touch|case|text|layout|postcards|sound`: the checks.
- `cargo fmt`: 140 columns (`rustfmt.toml`).
- `cd tools/emulator && cargo test --release`: the rules' tests, on the computer (the device has no test harness); `cargo clippy --release --all-targets -- -D warnings` there lints the shared code. Format with `rustfmt +stable --edition 2024 --check $(git ls-files '*.rs')`. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs all three in `test`, and builds the firmware in `firmware` (esp-rs/xtensa-toolchain, ESP-IDF cached, no `cfg.toml`, so no network settings): `cargo clippy --release --bins --lib -- -D warnings -A clippy::missing_const_for_thread_local` (the esp toolchain's clippy wrongly asks for a const initialiser lang.rs already has; `--all-targets` would try a test build, which the device can't have), then `cargo build --release`. A `v*` tag matching `Cargo.toml`'s version runs `release.yml`: the firmware merged into one unpadded image (`espflash save-image --merge --skip-padding`) on a GitHub release. `CONTRIBUTING.md`, the issue and pull request templates and `SECURITY.md` follow Carmen's.
- `cd tools/emulator && cargo run --release`: rerecord `docs/demo.gif` (needs ffmpeg; plays in real time, about two minutes).
